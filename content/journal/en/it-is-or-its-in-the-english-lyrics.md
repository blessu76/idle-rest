Spoken English shortens as it goes: "it is" becomes "it's", "do not" becomes "don't". A lyric can keep that casual sound or write the words out in full. We counted which one the English lyric sheets of dear(you); vol.1 (Version A) do. The music is made with AI (Suno) and the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## The method first

We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. We skipped section markers, whispered lines and the closing signature, which leaves the same 673 lines we used in [an earlier count of one-word lines](/journal/en/one-word-lines-in-ten-songs/). We counted every word that holds an apostrophe, then set the possessives aside ("today's", "someone's") and kept the contractions.

## 109 contractions, 21 forms

The sheets hold 115 words with an apostrophe, in 25 different forms and in 108 lines, 16.0% of all lines. Six of them are possessives: "else's" twice, "today's" twice, "someone's" and "tomorrow's" once each. The other 109 are contractions, in 21 forms.

| Contraction | Times |
|---|---|
| it's | 33 |
| don't | 13 |
| you're | 11 |
| let's | 11 |
| that's | 6 |
| I'm | 5 |
| wasn't, didn't | 4 each |
| you've | 4 |

"It's" is 30% of all contractions (33 of 109), and 28 of those 33 are "it's okay", the phrase we followed in [the post on "okay"](/journal/en/okay-counted-across-ten-songs/). The 13 "don't" are the same ones counted in [the post on "no" and "not"](/journal/en/no-and-not-in-ten-songs/).

## The written-out forms are nearly missing

| Short form | Times | Written out | Times |
|---|---|---|---|
| it's | 33 | it is | 0 |
| I'm | 5 | I am | 0 |
| don't | 13 | do not | 0 |
| wasn't, didn't, doesn't, haven't, isn't | 13 | was not, did not, does not, have not, is not | 0 |
| let's | 11 | let us | 0 |
| that's | 6 | that is | 1 |
| I'll, you'll, we'll, they'll | 4 | I will, we will | 3 |
| you're | 11 | you are | 4 |

Of the eight rows, five have no written-out form on the sheets at all. The single "that is" is split by a line break in song 09 ("Because even that / is more than enough."). The three written-out "will"s are "I will remember", "I will" and "I know we will." The four "you are" all end a clause: "who you are today" three times and "just as you are" once, a place where English does not allow "you're". So the short form is the rule on these sheets and the long form is the rare exception.

## Limits

The apostrophes follow the design documents, where a writer may have chosen a short form for the page and a singer may stretch or clip it. We counted only English lines of Version A, not whispers, and not the Korean lines. We cannot say whether the choice was planned. What the sheets show is a plainly spoken register: where English lets a word be short, the lines nearly always use the short form.
