Some lines in a lyric sheet are very short. A line made of a single word can get a breath to itself when it is sung. How often does that happen on dear(you); vol.1? We counted the one-word lines in the English lyric sheets (Version A) of all ten songs, after counting [the vocabulary](/journal/en/vocabulary-of-ten-english-lyric-sheets/) of the same sheets.

The method first. We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. We skipped section markers, whispered lines and the closing signature and counted every remaining line: 673 lines in all. A line counts as "one-word" if it holds exactly one English word, ignoring punctuation. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## 79 of 673

Seventy-nine lines are a single word, 11.7% of all lines. The average line is 3.79 words long (2,554 words over 673 lines), so a one-word line is about a quarter of the usual length.

Song 06, "You're Not Alone.", has the most with fifteen, which is one line in five. Song 08, "You Were Enough Today.", follows with thirteen. Song 05, "It's Okay to Rest a While.", has the fewest at four, and its lines are the second longest of the ten, 4.24 words each (only song 01 is longer, at 4.30). The ranking by one-word lines and the ranking by shortest average line are nearly the same: the two songs with the most one-word lines are also the two with the shortest average line.

## Which words stand alone

Of the 79 one-word lines, the repeated ones are: "today" nine times, "someone" eight, "now" six, "so" six, "tomorrow" five, "i" four and "but" four, then "ai" and "still" three each. Many of these are time words (today, now, tomorrow) and the person-word "someone". They are the same words that other posts follow through the album: [today](/journal/en/the-word-today-in-ten-songs/), the many "someone"s of [You're Not Alone.](/journal/en/six-someones-one-spoken-line/), and [tomorrow](/journal/en/counting-tomorrow-and-a-little/).

We do not claim the one-word lines were chosen to carry weight. The documents do not say why a word was set on its own line, and a line break in a design document is not a rest in the recording. What we can say is where they are: mostly on time words and on "someone".

## Limits

The counts depend on how we split lines, which follows the design documents. A recorded version may join or break lines differently. And we did not count Korean lines, where one-syllable lines are a different matter. Later in this series, we look at the punctuation marks of the sheets.
