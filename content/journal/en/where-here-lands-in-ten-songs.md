In track 07 of dear(you); vol.1, the word “here” does two jobs in consecutive sections. In the Pre-Chorus it marks how far the speaker has come. At the start of the Chorus it tells us that someone is present. We followed that small word across the ten English lyric sheets to see what else it can point to. The music is made with AI (Suno). The lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## Nine uses across five tracks

We read Version A of the ten song design sheets dated July 4, 2026. We left out headings, whispers, the closing signature and song titles. We then counted “here” as a word, regardless of capitalization, in the remaining 673 lines. Each of the nine occurrences is on a separate line. Tracks 01 and 05 have one each, tracks 02 and 10 have two each, and track 07 has three.

## A place beside someone

In the Bridge of track 01, “here beside you” puts the speaker next to the person being addressed. In track 05, “would you sit here with me?” asks the other person to come close. Track 02 does something similar in the Chorus with “sit right here.” Earlier in that track, Verse 1 puts “Here,” on a line of its own. The next two lines tell the other person that no one will rush them. The Chorus echoes that promise with an invitation to sit and stay beside someone.

## Two meanings in neighboring sections

Track 07 ends its Pre-Chorus with “all the way here today.” The lines just before it name the small kindnesses that carried the speaker through the day. Both the Chorus and the Final Chorus open with “I'm so glad you're here.” In those two places the word goes with “you.” The end of the Pre-Chorus marks how far the speaker has come. The openings of both choruses name the presence of someone else.

## The room and what stays there

The last track has “here” in each of its two verses. Verse 1 gives a location with “here in the room.” Verse 2 says “let's leave them here for now,” after naming the things that went well and the things that fell a little short. The first line locates the warm air still in the room. The second gives the day's good and difficult parts somewhere to stay for the evening.

## Limits

The count covers the English design sheets. It leaves open how the same ideas are written in Korean and whether the recording follows the printed lines. The descriptions of what “here” points to come from the nearby lines. A lyric sheet gives us the speaker's words, while the listener can picture the place for themselves.
