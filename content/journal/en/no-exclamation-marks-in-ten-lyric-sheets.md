Punctuation is the quietest part of a lyric sheet. Nobody sings a comma. Still, the marks show how the words were meant to be said on the page. In the design documents of dear(you); vol.1, one mark does not appear at all: the exclamation mark.

The method first. We searched the lyric blocks of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned. For the exclamation mark we searched both versions, English-main and Korean-main, whispers included, and found none in either one: zero. For the other marks we counted only the English lines of Version A, without whispers or the closing signature, 673 lines in all. What is sung may differ from the documents. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## Zero exclamation marks

Not one "!" in either version of any of the ten songs. That fits an album meant for the end of a tiring day, but the documents do not say it was a rule, so we only report the absence. The titles agree: nine of the ten English titles end with a period, and only the first one ends with a question mark, "Hard Day Again, Wasn't It?"

## What the lyrics use instead

| Song | Question marks | Dashes (—) | Ellipses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| 02 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| 03 | 2 | 5 | 1 |
| 04 | 2 | 4 | 0 |
| 05 | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| 06 | 0 | 3 | 1 |
| 07 | 1 | 5 | 0 |
| 08 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 09 | 2 | 4 | 0 |
| 10 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| **Total** | **14** | **29** | **5** |

Dashes are the most used mark of the three, 29 in all, and songs 03 and 07 have five each. Question marks are few: 14 across 673 lines. Song 05 asks the most, three times. Songs 06, 08 and 10 have no question mark at all. For the question-and-permission pattern in song 02 see [Tell Me, You Don't Have to Answer](/journal/en/tell-me-you-dont-have-to-answer/). Ellipses are the rarest, five in total, two of them in song 10.

## A link with the shortest lines

Songs 06 and 08 are the same two that have the most [one-word lines](/journal/en/one-word-lines-in-ten-songs/), fifteen and thirteen. A song made of many short statements has little room for questions. We note this as a coincidence in the numbers, not a rule: song 10 also has zero questions and only six one-word lines.

## Limits

Marks were counted as they appear in the design documents, where a line break or a dash may be a writing habit rather than an instruction to the singer. We did not count Korean punctuation beyond the exclamation mark. And a missing mark is not a missing feeling: a sentence can be warm without raising its voice.
