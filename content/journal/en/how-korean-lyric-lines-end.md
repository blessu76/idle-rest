In Korean, the end of a line carries a great deal. The last syllable can make a sentence polite or plain, a statement or a question, finished or still open. So we looked at how the Korean lines of dear(you); vol.1 end. These are the Korean-main versions (Version B) in the ten design documents, written on 2026-07-04, 679 lines and 4,041 Hangul syllables in all, which is 5.95 syllables per line. As always, what is sung may differ from the documents. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person. Whispers are not counted.

## The last syllable, counted

We took the final Hangul syllable of each line, ignoring punctuation. The most common ones:

| Last syllable | Lines | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 도 | 80 | 11.8% |
| 어 | 59 | 8.7% |
| 은 | 55 | 8.1% |
| 아 | 48 | 7.1% |
| 는 | 37 | 5.4% |
| 고 | 33 | 4.9% |
| 까 | 26 | 3.8% |
| 야 | 24 | 3.5% |

The leader, 도, is the particle that means "also" or "even." Lines like "today too" end that way. Next come 어 and 아, and the particles 은 and 는, which mark a topic. Several of these are not sentence endings in the strict sense. A line that ends on a particle or a connective, like 고 ("and"), leaves the thought open and hands it to the next line.

## What is rare

Only 15 of 679 lines end in 다, the plain written-style ending, at most three in any one song. And the polite 요 ending appears exactly three times: "오늘도 고생했어요." in song 04, "오늘도 고생 많으셨어요." in song 06, and "아직 부족해요." in song 08. That is 0.4% of the lines. These songs mostly speak in the informal register, and the polite form is rare.

The question ending 까 is the next to note: 26 lines, with songs 03 and 05 having five each and songs 09 and 10 four each. For how the English lines handle questions, see [No Exclamation Marks in Ten Lyric Sheets](/journal/en/no-exclamation-marks-in-ten-lyric-sheets/).

## At a glance

In short: the most common last syllable is 도, at 80 lines. The plain 다 ends 15 lines, the question 까 ends 26, and the polite 요 appears three times. The 679 lines average 5.95 syllables, so they are short and the last syllable carries a lot of weight. Of the eight syllables in the table above, few close a sentence firmly, and several are particles or syllables that lead into the next line. So the Korean lines in these ten songs read as continuing more than concluding.

## Reading it carefully

The design documents do not explain why the lines end as they do, so we do not claim an intention. What the numbers support is only this: the Korean lines lean on open, connecting endings far more than on closed, polite ones. Last-syllable counting is also crude. 어 can end an informal sentence or a connected clause, and we did not separate those. Treat the table as a map of where the lines stop, not a grammar analysis.
