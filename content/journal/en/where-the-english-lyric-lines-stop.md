Nobody sings a full stop, yet a lyric sheet is full of them. The last mark on a line tells the reader whether the thought is finished or handed on to the next line. We looked at how the lines of dear(you); vol.1 stop in the English lyric sheets (Version A): with a period, a comma, another mark or nothing at all. The music is made with AI (Suno) and the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## The method first

We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. We skipped section markers, whispered lines and the closing signature, which leaves the same 673 lines we used in [an earlier count of one-word lines](/journal/en/one-word-lines-in-ten-songs/). For each line we looked only at its last character. Question marks, dashes and ellipses were already counted in [the post on exclamation marks](/journal/en/no-exclamation-marks-in-ten-lyric-sheets/), so here they are grouped as "other".

## Three endings do most of the work

| Last mark of the line | Lines | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Period | 265 | 39.4% |
| No mark | 207 | 30.8% |
| Comma | 150 | 22.3% |
| Dash (—) | 28 | 4.2% |
| Question mark | 12 | 1.8% |
| Closing quotation mark | 6 | 0.9% |
| Ellipsis (…) | 5 | 0.7% |
| **All lines** | **673** | **100%** |

A period closes a line about once in every two and a half lines. The other endings, a comma, a dash or no mark, are all ways of saying the sentence is not over yet. Together they make up 385 lines, 57.2% of the sheets, more than the 265 that stop at a period.

Song 03, "I Was Scared, Too.", stops at a period most often, 35 of 78 lines (44.9%), and it also has the fewest comma endings, only 7 (9.0%). Song 06, "You're Not Alone.", has the most commas, 21 of 76 lines (27.6%), and song 08, "You Were Enough Today.", is close behind with 20 of 74 (27.0%). These are the same two songs that have the most one-word lines, so short lines that lean on one another look like a habit of those sheets, not an accident of one song.

## A period is a real stop

The strongest pattern is about what comes next. Of the 255 lines that end in a period and are not the last line of a song, none is followed by a line that begins in lowercase: every one of them is followed by a capital letter. The other way round, a line that ends in a comma is followed by a lowercase line 143 times out of 150, and a line with no mark at the end 200 times out of 207. So in these sheets the last mark is a fairly reliable signal: a period means a new sentence, and anything else means the sentence goes on. The longest run of lines with no ending mark at all is four.

## Limits

The marks follow the design documents, where a line break or a comma may be a writing habit and not an instruction to the singer, and a recorded version may join or split the lines differently. We counted only English lines of Version A, not whispers, and not the Korean lines, which end on syllables rather than marks (see [how Korean lyric lines end](/journal/en/how-korean-lyric-lines-end/)). We also cannot say whether the period-or-comma choice was planned. What the sheets show is that the lines mostly stop where the sentence stops, and that more lines hand the thought on than close it.
