How does one lyric skeleton repeat across all ten songs of *dear(you); vol.1*, idle()'s first album? Open the lyric drafts one after another and the same shape keeps coming back. This post lays the whole skeleton out by track number, going by the drafts, and the song-by-song posts link back here instead of repeating it. What is actually sung on the recordings can be shorter than the draft.

One thing up front: the music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person. We would rather say so from the first line.

## Six slots, and a seventh

Every lyric follows the same order: Verse 1, Verse 2, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Outro. Tracks 1 (S1A1.01) and 2 (S1A1.02) stop at those six. Track 1 runs 11, 12, 8, 12, 9 and 7 lines across them, 59 in all. Instead of singing the chorus once more, it moves straight to a greeting: "thank you for making it this far today."

From track 3 through track 10, a Final Chorus sits between the Bridge and the Outro, making seven. The first song to get that slot is track 3, "I Was Scared, Too." Why the change was made isn't written down anywhere. What we can confirm is that on track 4, "You're Still Shining.", the Final Chorus doesn't just repeat the chorus. It is rewritten with new lines, and it ends on this one:

> Today, too, you're still shining.

## Counting the slots

Two songs with six slots and eight with seven make 68 lyric slots per version. The English and Korean versions share slot names and order in all ten songs, and 55 of the 68 slots even have the same number of lines. The other 13 differ by exactly one. Matching slots don't mean matching length: the Korean drafts of tracks 3 to 10 run 82, 82, 68, 78, 69, 80, 70 and 74 lines, and track 5 is the shortest.

## English and Korean, one blueprint

Each song is made in two versions: one with English as the main language (female vocal), one with Korean as the main language (male vocal). Both versions share the same sections, and their line counts differ by 0 to 1 per section. One version runs 56 to 82 lines, counting the whispers and the closing signature (titles and blank lines excluded). The English side is adapted, not translated word for word, because literal translation breaks the rhythm and the feeling.

A small device ties the two versions together: the whisper. The English version carries a Korean word or two, and the Korean version carries an English one. In the English version of track 1, a whispered *괜찮아…* ("it's okay") appears inside the first verse. Whispers sit in the Verse 1 and Chorus of all ten songs, never in a Pre-Chorus. Each version has 29, in the same slots.

Track 3 shows the pairing most clearly. The English version whispers *괜찮아…*, *걱정 마…* ("don't worry") and *천천히…* ("slowly") in Verse 1, the Chorus and the Final Chorus. The Korean version whispers *It's okay...*, *Don't worry...* and *Take your time...* in the same three places. Only the language and the voice change.

## The last line of every song

Every Outro, in both languages, ends on the same line: a one-breath whisper, fixed in English, "ipe. be still". Unlike the other whispers, this one stays in English in both versions. It is the sound version of the brand line `ipe(); // be still`, meant to work like a logo you hear.
