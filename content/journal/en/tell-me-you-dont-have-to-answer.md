The song design document for the second song of *dear(you); vol.1*, "It's Okay, Take Your Time." (Korean title 「괜찮아, 천천히 말해봐.」), states its theme like this (our translation): a song where being listened to until the end is itself the comfort, more than being consoled. Yet the title asks you to speak, and the chorus says you needn't answer. This post counts where those two things sit in the lyric sheets.

First, the scope. We read the lyric sheets in the song design document (Korean and English versions) and its style notes. That document was written on 2026-07-04 when the song was requested, so what is actually sung may differ, and we did not check it against the audio. Everything said about sound is a requested spec. The music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person.

## The questions end in the first verse

Count the question marks. The Korean sheet has one: 「어떤 하루였어?」 ("What kind of day was it?"), the fourth line of Verse 1. The English sheet has two: "did today turn out to be?" and "it hurt a little, didn't it?", a tag question where the Korean line is a plain statement. Both are in Verse 1. From Verse 2 to the end there is no question mark. The song asks once, then stops asking and waits.

## The sentences that give permission

The chorus appears once; the final chorus that some other songs have is not in this sheet. The first three lines of the Korean chorus are:

> 천천히 말해봐.
> 난 끝까지 들을게.
> 대답하지 않아도 괜찮아.

Roughly: "Take your time and tell me. / I'll listen to the very end. / You don't have to answer." Right after the invitation to speak comes permission not to. Then come "It's okay to cry for a little while" and "You can just sit here and stay beside me" (in the Korean, 「잠시 울어도 괜찮아」 and 「여기 앉아서 같이 있어도 괜찮아」). Sentences ending in 괜찮아 ("it's okay") occur five times in the Korean sheet: once in Verse 1, three in the chorus and once in the Bridge. All three in the chorus say that you may leave something undone. None tells you to do something. We read the Bridge line "I can wait for you" as the same attitude, moved into time.

## Giving the doubt a seat

Verse 2 writes down a thought the listener might have: "Maybe it was me who fell short." The song does not argue with it. It answers, "everyone has it once in a while." Then the Pre-Chorus repeats "enough" three times: you thought it through enough, tried hard enough, held on more than enough. The Korean sheet repeats 「충분히」 the same three times. The order is: let the thought be said, then say it was enough. Whether this was meant is not in the documents, so we only record that the order is this.

## What the sound spec shows

The tempo field says 70 BPM. The instrument field lists Soft Piano, Warm Guitar, Gentle Pad, Light Lo-fi Beat and Coffee Shop Ambience. The last entry names a place, not a sound. You could read the chorus line "sit right here" as a scene in such a place, but that is two lines set side by side, not something the document says.

## Where the whispers cross

In the Korean version the whispers are English: "It's okay..." and "Stay with me...". In the English version the same spots are whispered in Korean, 「괜찮아…」 and 「같이 있어…」. The design document says each version gets the opposite language as a whisper. In this song the same two phrases going back and forth between two languages is the clearest case.

## What we are not claiming

- Why the questions were cut to one, or the permissions gathered in the chorus, is not in the documents. We read the result of counting.
- We did not check the real tempo, instruments or sung words in the audio.
- "A song that listens" is the theme sentence of the design document. It says nothing about how a listener will experience it.
