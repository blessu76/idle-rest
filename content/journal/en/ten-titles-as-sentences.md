Most track lists are made of labels: a noun, a place, a color. The track list of *dear(you); vol.1* is made of sentences. Each of the ten titles is something a person could say out loud to a friend at the end of a long day. This post does not read the lyrics. It reads only the ten titles, the way you would read a short poem: who is speaking, when it is happening, and in what tone.

Our scope is narrow. The titles come from the album page on this site and the album plan, and we counted only what is written in the titles themselves. We did not measure anything in the recordings. The music is made with AI (Suno), and the lyrics are AI-drafted and checked by a person.

## Ten titles, ten sentences

| # | English title | What the sentence does | Tense |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Hard Day Again, Wasn't It? | asks | past |
| 02 | It's Okay, Take Your Time. | invites | imperative |
| 03 | I Was Scared, Too. | confesses | past |
| 04 | You're Still Shining. | reassures | present |
| 05 | It's Okay to Rest a While. | permits | present |
| 06 | You're Not Alone. | states | present |
| 07 | I'm Glad You're Here. | thanks | present |
| 08 | You Were Enough Today. | affirms | past |
| 09 | Let's Smile a Little Tomorrow. | proposes | toward tomorrow |
| 10 | You Did So Well Today. | praises | past |

The middle column is our own label for each sentence, not a term from the album plan. Only the first title is a question, and it is the only one that ends in a question mark. The other nine end in a period.

## Where the tenses sit

Count the tenses and you get four past, four present, one invitation and one that looks ahead. The order is the interesting part. Tracks 01 and 03 look back at the day, track 02 invites, and then tracks 04 to 07 are four present-tense sentences in a row: you're still shining, it's okay to rest, you're not alone, I'm glad you're here. After that the album looks back once more at track 08, turns toward tomorrow at track 09, and closes with a past-tense praise at track 10.

We read this as the middle of the album being the part that happens now, in the room, while the first and last songs talk about the day that has just ended. The album plan does not say the titles were arranged by tense, so treat that as a reading and not as a design note.

## Who is speaking

"You" appears in six of the ten titles (02, 04, 06, 07, 08 and 10). "I" appears in two, tracks 03 and 07, and track 07 is the only title that holds both. "We" appears once, as the "Let's" of track 09. Track 03 is the only title where the speaker talks about their own past, and its last word, "too," ties that fear straight back to the listener. Everywhere else the sentence is about you.

## The time words

Titles also carry the day itself. Track 01 says "Hard Day Again," track 02 says "Take Your Time," track 05 says "a While," tracks 08 and 10 both say "Today," and track 09 says "Tomorrow." In the Korean titles, 오늘도 ("today, too") opens tracks 01, 08 and 10. So the track list moves from a hard day, through time given back, to today being enough, and then to tomorrow.

## The Korean titles speak casually

All ten Korean titles end in the casual register people use with a close friend, such as 힘들었지, 무서웠어 and 고생했어, and none uses a polite or formal ending. That choice is visible in the text. Why it was made is not written in the documents we read, so we only note it.

## Where the two versions part

Most English titles map closely to the Korean ones, but two stand out. Track 02's Korean title, 괜찮아, 천천히 말해봐., includes an invitation to talk ("try telling me, slowly"), while the English title, "It's Okay, Take Your Time.", keeps only the slowness. Track 10's Korean title, 오늘도... 정말 고생했어., has an ellipsis and a word, 고생, for effort and hardship that does not carry into a single English word. The English "You Did So Well Today." keeps the praise and drops both. The album plan says the English side is adapted, not translated word for word, so these are what that rule looks like in two titles.

## What we are not claiming

The album plan states one link between titles and songs: the chorus hook is the title. Beyond that, the tense pattern, the speakers and the time words above are what we found by counting, and the documents do not say they were planned. A reader can lay the ten sentences out in order and make up their own mind.
