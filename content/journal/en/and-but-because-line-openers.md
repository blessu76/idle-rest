Teachers tell us not to begin a sentence with "and" or "but". Lyrics do it all the time, because a line is not always a sentence: it can pick up the thought the last line left open. We counted the lines of dear(you); vol.1 that begin with a small connecting word, in the English lyric sheets (Version A) of all ten songs. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## The method first

We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. We skipped section markers, whispered lines and the closing signature, which leaves 673 lines, the same set as in [the count of one-word lines](/journal/en/one-word-lines-in-ten-songs/). We looked at the first word of each line, ignoring capital letters, and counted four connectors: and, but, because, so.

## 70 of 673

| First word | Lines |
|---|---|
| and | 28 |
| but | 21 |
| because | 12 |
| so | 9 |
| **Total** | **70** |

That is 10.4% of all lines, about one in ten. "And" is the most common of the four. We also looked for "or", "while" and "until" and found none: no line in the sheets begins with them. The sheets add things (and), turn them round (but), give a reason (because) and conclude (so), but they do not offer alternatives.

Every song has at least five such lines. Songs 09 and 10 have the most, nine each, and song 09 alone holds seven of the 28 "and" lines, a quarter of them. Song 07 has no "and" line and song 05 has no "but" line. Song 01 is the only one without any "because" or "so" line, and it is also the one with the fewest connector lines, five (shared with song 07).

## Lines that lean on the last line

Most of these lines are continuations, not new starts. Across the sheets 374 of the 673 lines (55.6%) begin with a lowercase letter, which in the design documents marks a line that carries on from the one before; the connectors are a clear part of that group. A few of the four words also stand alone: our count of one-word lines found "but" four times and "so" six times, so some of the 70 are a connector with nothing after it.

## Limits

We counted first words only, not what follows, and "so" can be a connector ("so I stay") or an intensifier ("so slow"); we did not separate the two. Capital letters and line breaks follow the design documents, and a recorded version may join or break lines differently. We did not count Korean lines, which carry connectors at the end of a clause rather than at the start. We cannot say whether the connectors were chosen on purpose. What the sheets show is that about one line in ten begins by leaning on the line before it.
