When you see a code like S1A1.03 on a song, the first two parts name a season and an album. This post is about the "season" part: how the studio plans to lay out its albums over one day. We have mentioned the idea twice in passing, once in [How an AI Lo-Fi Song Gets Made](/journal/en/how-a-song-gets-made/) and once in [The Six Series of idle()](/journal/en/six-series-of-idle/). Here we put it in one place and say plainly which parts are released and which are only planned.

## The plan in one table

ipe() calls its album structure the Album Universe. Season 1 covers a developer's full daily cycle, with one album for each time of day.

| Code | Album | Time of day | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1A1 | *dear(you); vol.1* | Night, before sleep | Released, 10 of 10 tracks |
| S1A2 | *Breathe. Code. Rest.* | Dawn, early morning | In planning |
| S1A3 | *Quiet Commits* | Morning, deep focus | In planning |
| S1A4 | *After Deployment* | Evening, after work | In planning |

The order is a day read forward from the night before sleep: night, dawn, morning, evening. The first album is described as "ten letters to the you who made it through another hard day." That sentence is the description on the site, not our interpretation.

## What is released, and what is not

Only the first row is out. All ten songs of *dear(you); vol.1* exist in an English version and a Korean version, with codes from S1A1.01 to S1A1.10. The other three albums are listed as in planning. We have no track lists, release dates or sound documents for them, so we do not describe how they will sound. The titles above are the planned titles as written on the site, and a planned title can still change.

Counting everything released so far gives twelve tracks: the ten album songs plus two singles, "good morning, world" (U-001) and "still compiling" (N-001). Singles carry a series letter instead of an album address, and the series post explains why.

## The day already moves inside the first album

A night album does not stay at one hour. The sound section of the album plan says the ten songs move from night toward morning, and it splits the move into six stretches: songs 01 and 02, 03 and 04, 05 alone, 06 to 08, 09 and 10. Song 09 carries very faint morning birds, and the note beside them describes this as a hint of the move from night to morning. We checked this in the documents. We did not measure it in the recordings.

So the season-level order (night, then dawn, then morning, then evening) and the order inside the first album (night leaning toward morning) point the same way. We read that as a sign the plan was drawn from the day's direction, but the documents do not say that was the reason, so it is only our reading.

## Where the time of day shows up

The one place where the plan reaches the listener directly is the visuals. The visual stage takes the tone of the time of day as an input, so songs on the same album share the colors of one hour. The fixed parts do not change from album to album: a palette centered on deep navy, warm gold, sage green and cream, and a monospaced typeface that recalls a code editor. How the dawn, morning and evening albums will apply this has not been decided in any document we have.

## What the address tells you

Because the album number is the time of day, the address carries a little information on its own. S1A1.03 is the third song of the first album, which is a night album. S1A3.xx, when it exists, will be a morning song. We say "will" only about the naming, and not about when it arrives.

The studio's stated direction is to release singles one at a time and to gather them into an album when there are enough. That is how the first album was built. Nothing in the repository says how or when the other three will come out, and we will not guess.
