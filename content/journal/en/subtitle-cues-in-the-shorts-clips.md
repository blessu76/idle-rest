Every Shorts clip from dear(you); vol.1 has a subtitle file next to it, a plain SRT list of cues, each with a start, an end and a line of text. We have written before about [where the Shorts clip starts](/journal/en/where-the-shorts-clip-starts/) and about [how much of the draft reaches the screen](/journal/en/how-much-of-the-draft-reaches-the-screen/). This time we look at the cues themselves: how many, how long, and how much of the 58 seconds they fill. The music is made with AI (Suno) and the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person. These counts come from the subtitle files, not from watching each video.

## The English clips

There is one English clip file per song, each running about 58 seconds. Cue lengths are end minus start; words are counted in the cue text.

Across the ten clips, subtitles are on screen for about 521 of roughly 580 seconds, and 890 words are shown, about 1.7 words per second of cue time. The clips differ more in how they cut the time than in how much they show. Song 05 has the fewest cues, six, and the longest single cue at 10.9 seconds. Song 04 has the most, 18, and its longest is only 5.2 seconds. Several short lines in a row, or a few long ones that stay up for a long time: both fit in the same minute.

## The Korean clips

Korean files exist for songs 02 to 10, and for songs 02 and 03 there are two variants with different start points. Cue counts range from 7 (songs 08 and 09) to 13 (song 07, and one variant of song 03 at 12). Korean cues are longer on average than the English ones, and in seven of the nine songs they are fewer: song 04 has 9 Korean cues against 18 English, song 08 has 7 against 11. The pairs use different clip starts, so this is a comparison of two edits, not of two translations.

## What to take from it

A clip is not a lyric sheet in miniature. The cue lengths show editing choices, such as holding a line for ten seconds in song 05, that the lyric draft alone does not. A viewer who reads quickly sees a different clip from one who waits for the next line. We do not know how people actually watch these clips, and the files do not say.
