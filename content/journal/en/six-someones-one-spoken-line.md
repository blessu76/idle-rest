The design document for the sixth song of *dear(you); vol.1*, "You're Not Alone." (Korean title 「혼자가 아니야.」), states its theme like this (our summary): a day you thought you got through alone, though people were quietly walking beside you; a song about the warm power of "together." This post counts one small word in the lyric sheet, "someone." It looks at what each of those someones does, and whose words, if anyone's, are quoted directly.

First, the scope. We read the lyric sheet in the song's design document (Korean and English versions) and its style notes. The document was written on 2026-07-04 when the song was commissioned, so what is actually sung may differ. Everything said about sound is a requested specification; what we compared against the subtitles, and the fact that we did not compare against the audio, are described below. The music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person.

## Six someones

In the Korean lyric sheet, 「누군가」 ("someone") appears exactly six times.

| Where | Which someone | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 2 | first | leaves a review without a word |
| Verse 2 | second | hands you a cup of coffee |
| Verse 2 | third | leaves a small word: "you worked hard again today" |
| Chorus | fourth | was cheering you on |
| Final Chorus | fifth | wished for you to smile |
| Final Chorus | sixth | wished for you to rest |

The English sheet has one more. In Verse 1: "You wanted, just a little, to lean on someone." That is a wish with nobody attached yet. The Korean line, 「조금은 기대고 싶었는데」, has no such word, so the count is seven in English and six in Korean.

Read the arrangement and the first three do things you can see at a desk, while the last three are things you cannot see. In Verse 2 the sheet says "without a word" about the first, says nothing about words for the second, who simply hands over coffee, and only the third leaves words. The chorus turns this into cheering, and the final chorus into wishes. You could read it as a move from what was done to what was held in the heart. That is our reading; the design document does not say the lines were arranged that way.

## Where speech is quoted

Quotation marks appear twice in the sheet. The first, in Verse 1, is the singer's own mask, "I'm okay." (「괜찮습니다.」), said while waiting for the elevator. The second, in Verse 2, is "you worked hard again today" (「오늘도 고생 많으셨어요.」), and it is the only place where one of the six someones is quoted directly. These are also the only two lines written in the polite register in the Korean sheet. Every other line is casual speech between people who are close. So workplace speech is written in a workplace register, and what the song itself says to you is written in a friend's. That, too, is only a reading; the document gives no reason.

The word 괜찮 ("okay") deserves the same look. The root appears six times in the Korean sheet. The first two are the mask: 「괜찮은 척 웃었지」 and 「괜찮습니다」. The other four are permission: 「솔직해져도 괜찮아」, 「잠시 기대도 괜찮아」, 「그래도 괜찮아」 and 「조금 기대도 괜찮아」. The English sheet is shaped a little differently. The mask side is "you smiled like you were fine" and "I'm okay.", and "okay" on the permission side appears four times ("it's okay" three times, "that's okay" once). The split into mask and permission is the same, but it does not map one-to-one onto the Korean 2 + 4.

## I, you, we

Setting the whispers aside, the English sheet has "I" five times: "I'm okay." in Verse 1 (inside the quotation marks), "I… I wasn't alone after all." in Verse 2, and "I thought I was holding on alone" in the pre-chorus (two). "Me" appears once, in "beside me." From the chorus on there is no "I" and no "me." The Korean sheet likewise has no first-person singular after 「나…」 in Verse 2 and 「내 옆」 in the pre-chorus. After the chorus it is "you," and the first "we" arrives in the bridge: "we lived through the same day, together" (Korean 「우리」). Two more "we"s come in the final chorus, and the outro, after "let's walk together.", trails off into Slowly. / Little by little. / Together.

## "Alone" is always negated

In the Korean sheet 「혼자」 appears four times, and every time it is negated or taken back: 「혼자가 아니었구나」, 「혼자 버틴 줄 알았는데」, and 「혼자가 아니야」 in the chorus and the final chorus. The English is the same: "I wasn't alone after all," "I thought I was holding on alone, but…" and "You're not alone." (twice). A raw count of "alone" in the English sheet gives five, but the bridge's "that alone" means "just that," so we leave it out. The loneliness of Verse 1 is written without this word at all. There are only two masks and a wish to lean.

## What the sound spec shows

The tempo is 70 BPM and the genre is Ambient Lo-fi · Emotional Chill. The instrument field lists Soft Felt Piano, Warm Acoustic Guitar, Gentle Strings, Ambient Pads, Vinyl Crackle and Soft Rain Texture. The English version is a sweet female vocal with Korean whispers (「괜찮아…」, 「같이 있어…」); the Korean version is a warm male vocal with English whispers ("It's okay...", "Stay with me..."). The final chorus gets a harmony from the opposite gender, and both versions end on the whisper `ipe. be still`. The mix field has four entries, and one of them is Minimal Dynamics. All of this is the requested specification.

## What we are not claiming

- Counting the "someone"s is a way of reading. Whether this arrangement was intended is not in the document.
- The move from deeds to heartfelt wishes, and the workplace register against a friend's, are our readings.
- The studio repository holds short caption files for this song. They cover the end of Verse 2 through the chorus (the English one also reaches the start of the bridge), and the lines read the same as the sheet apart from one typo in the English captions. The Korean one includes 「오늘도 고생 많으셨어요」. But captions are text, and we did not listen to the audio to compare.
