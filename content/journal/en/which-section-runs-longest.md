Every song of dear(you); vol.1 follows the same order of sections, which we laid out in [Ten Letters, One Skeleton](/journal/en/ten-letters-one-skeleton/). That post is about the order. This one asks a different question: how long is each slot, in lines? We assumed the chorus, the part people remember, would be the longest. The numbers say otherwise.

The method first. We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. This time we counted lines per section and left out whispered lines and the closing signature, so our numbers can be a little smaller than the ones in the skeleton post, which counts whispers. In all, 673 lines are counted. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## Average lines per section

| Section | Songs | Total lines | Average | Shortest | Longest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | 10 | 121 | 12.1 | 10 | 15 |
| Verse 2 | 10 | 114 | 11.4 | 10 | 15 |
| Bridge | 10 | 114 | 11.4 | 8 | 15 |
| Final Chorus | 8 | 82 | 10.3 | 9 | 12 |
| Chorus | 10 | 99 | 9.9 | 8 | 12 |
| Pre-Chorus | 10 | 72 | 7.2 | 5 | 9 |
| Outro | 10 | 71 | 7.1 | 5 | 8 |

The first verse is the longest slot on average, and the Chorus comes only fifth of seven. The Bridge ties Verse 2 at 11.4 lines. The Pre-Chorus and the Outro are the short ones, around seven lines each. Final Chorus exists in eight songs (tracks 3 to 10), which is why its total is smaller. The Chorus and the Final Chorus together hold 181 of the 673 lines, which is 26.9%.

## The chorus shrinks, the bridge grows

Choruses by track, from 01 to 10, run 11, 10, 12, 12, 8, 10, 9, 9, 9 and 9 lines. The first four average 11.25. The last four are all exactly nine. Bridges go the other way: 8, 8, 13, 11, 12, 14, 11, 15, 11 and 11 lines, so the first two songs have the shortest bridges (eight lines each) and song 08 has the longest (fifteen).

Why the later songs settle on a nine-line chorus is not written in the documents, and we do not guess. What we can say is the pattern in the drafts: the later the song, the less the chorus changes length. It is a pattern in the design documents, not a claim about what listeners hear, and it says nothing about whether a longer section feels longer, since a slow line takes more time than a fast one. For the tempo of each song, see [Tempo Ladder](/journal/en/tempo-ladder-of-ten-songs/).

## What to take from it

If you want the part of an idle() song with the most words, look at the first verse, not the chorus. The chorus is shorter than both verses and the bridge, and perhaps that is the point of a chorus: say less, and say it again. Next, a few of the smallest marks in the sheets.
