Song 08 of dear(you); vol.1 is titled "You Were Enough Today." One word of that title turns up across the whole album. We counted "enough" in the English lyric sheets (Version A) and looked at where it stands. The music is made with AI (Suno) and the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## The method first

We read the English lyric section of the ten song design documents, written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ. We skipped section markers, whispered lines and the closing signature, which leaves the same 673 lines we used in [an earlier count of one-word lines](/journal/en/one-word-lines-in-ten-songs/). We counted the word "enough" wherever it stood in a line.

## Twenty times in eight songs

"Enough" appears 20 times, each in its own line, in eight of the ten songs. Songs 06 and 07 do not have it.

The three chorus-type sections hold 253 of the 673 lines, 37.6%, but 18 of the 20 "enough"s, 90%. Verse 1 and the Outro have none.

## What is "enough"

The phrase "more than enough" appears 7 times, in six songs (05 has two). Most of the others are short statements about the day: "this far is enough" (songs 01 and 05), "you were enough" (songs 03, 04 and twice in 08), "you were already enough" (song 08), "that alone is enough" (song 09). In 15 of the 20 cases the word "today" is within two lines of it in the same song, so it is usually "enough" for a day. 19 of the 20 lines end on the word itself.

## The one "not enough"

In song 08, Verse 2, a line is put in quotation marks: "it's not enough yet", described as the first thing a person says even when praised. It is the one negative use among the 20, and the song's Chorus, two sections later, says "you were enough" (more on song 08 in [the post on its two lists](/journal/en/two-lists-one-day/)).

## Limits

The lines follow the design documents, where a word may land differently in the recorded version. We counted only English lines of Version A, not whispers, and not the Korean lines, so we cannot say how the Korean sheets handle the same idea. We also cannot say whether the word was placed on purpose. What the sheets show is that "enough" appears in eight of ten songs and mostly in the sections that repeat.
