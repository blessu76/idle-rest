Each of the ten song design documents of *dear(you); vol.1* has a metadata table, and one row of it is called 테마, "theme". It is a single line. This post puts the ten lines side by side and notices one thing about their grammar: in seven of them, the sentence first names something to put down, and only then says what the song does instead.

First, the scope. We read only the theme row in each song's design document. The documents were written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, and they state intentions, not the audio we have heard. The translations below are ours, and the sorting into groups is our reading, not a label from the documents. The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## Seven lines that put something down

| Song | What the theme line sets aside (our translation) | What it puts in its place |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | "without a solution" | simply asking how you are, first |
| 02 | "rather than consolation" | "listening to the end" itself becoming the consolation |
| 03 | not trying to remove the fear | acknowledging it first: "me too" |
| 04 | not "I have to win" | "you are still precious" |
| 07 | not "the person who does well" | "the person who stays beside you" |
| 08 | the habit of recalling what you failed at first, set down | telling yourself, for the first time, "you worked hard" |
| 10 | "without advice or encouragement" | the warmest goodbye, "Sleep well." |

The Korean markers are 없이 (without) in songs 01 and 10, 위로보다 (rather than consolation) in 02, 없애려 않고 (not trying to remove) in 03, 아니라 (not X but Y) in 04 and 07, and 내려놓고 (setting down) in 08. The word 없이 is used in two theme lines only, the first song's and the last song's. The word 아니라 is used in two, songs 04 and 07, the two songs that share the bridge refrain, which [The Same Sentence in Two Songs](/journal/en/same-sentence-in-two-songs/) reads.

## Three lines that do not

Songs 05, 06 and 09 are written differently. Song 05's line states a permission without putting anything down: "Resting is also courage — 'for today at least, it's okay to pause.'" Song 06 and song 09 turn on a concession, not on an omission. Song 06 says "I thought I was holding on alone, but there were people quietly walking with me — the warm power of 'together.'" Song 09 says "The world will be busy tomorrow too, but may we smile a little more tomorrow — a small hope." Both start from the hard fact and then add a "but".

## Reading the pattern with care

It is tempting to say that the album is built on "not X, but Y", but the table cannot carry that much. The lines are one-sentence summaries written for the songs' commissioning, and the same documents hold longer story and mood fields that say more. We also counted only grammatical markers. We did not count themes that are implicitly set against something, and a different reader could sort 02's 위로보다 differently, since it is a comparison, not a plain negation.

What the table does support is smaller. In the first song (without a solution) and the last song (without advice or encouragement), the document puts the first and last themes in the same grammatical form. [Reading the First and Last Songs as a Pair](/journal/en/open-with-a-question-close-with-comfort/) reads those two songs together from other evidence. Here, only the wording of the theme row is the same.

## What we are not claiming

- That the theme lines were written to a pattern. The documents do not say so.
- That seven is a count of anything but our own sorting of ten sentences.
- Anything about the sung lyrics or the audio. We read only the theme row.
- Anything about how a listener will feel. No medical or other effect is claimed.
