All ten songs on *dear(you); vol.1* are made in two versions. According to the album plan, the English-main version has a sweet female vocal and the Korean-main version a gentle male one. Both versions carry the same rule: whatever language is not the main one goes in as a whisper. In the English version the whispers are Korean, and in the Korean version they are English. This post follows that rule through the lyric drafts, counting the whispers of all ten songs one by one.

A note on scope first. What we read is the lyric draft, and what is actually sung on a recording can differ from the draft. How quiet each whisper is on the recording, and on which beat it lands, is outside what this post has checked.

And one more disclosure. The music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person. The drafts discussed here were made that way too.

## The rule is simple

The production table in the album plan splits the two versions like this.

- English version: English is the main language, in natural paraphrase, never a literal translation. Korean is added as a whispered accent, and the vocal is a sweet female voice.
- Korean version: Korean (the original text) is the main language, with English added as a whispered accent. The vocal is a gentle male voice.

The examples in the table are short. For the English version the Korean words listed are 괜찮아 ("it's okay"), 천천히 ("slowly") and 같이 있어 ("stay with me"); for the Korean version the English words are "It's okay" and "Stay with me". There is one exception: the very last line of every song, "ipe. be still", is fixed in English in both versions.

The per-song style notes ask for the same thing. The wording varies, but the notes of all ten songs ask for the opposite language as a whisper in each version. The notes for tracks 03 and 04 place a soft harmony in the opposite gender in the chorus, and the notes for tracks 06 to 10 place it in the final chorus. The note for track 05 asks for the opposite-gender harmony without naming a place. How that harmony sounds on the recordings has not been checked.

## Why the opposite language? It is not on record

The reason is not recorded. The album plan and the song drafts stop at the rule and its examples. They do not explain why the whisper goes in the other language instead of the same one. So this post does not invent a reason. It only states what follows from the structure.

Either version reads through on its own, because every full sentence stays in the main language. The whisper is a single phrase set into a gap between lines, so the story does not break for someone who does not know its language. Someone who reads both versions gets something else: at the same spot in the same song, the same meaning arrives once in Korean and once in English. That is what the rule produces, and no more.

## One song as a mirror: "I Was Scared, Too."

![A diagram of the three whispers of S1A1.03 in both versions. The English-main version whispers 괜찮아, 걱정 마 and 천천히; the Korean-main version whispers It's okay, Don't worry and Take your time, in the same three places: Verse 1, Chorus and Final Chorus. The main-language line sung right after each whisper is shown in small type](/assets/journal/whispers-in-the-other-language/whisper-mirror-track03.jpg)

*The three whispers of S1A1.03. The large type is the whisper; the small type is the line that follows it in the main language.*

Take the third song, "I Was Scared, Too." (Korean title: 나도 무서웠어.), and set it in front of a mirror. It has three whispers. In the English version they are:

> 괜찮아…  (Verse 1, "it's okay")
> 걱정 마…  (Chorus, "don't worry")
> 천천히…  (Final Chorus, "slowly")

In the Korean version the same three places hold English:

> It's okay...  (Verse 1)
> Don't worry...  (Chorus)
> Take your time...  (Final Chorus)

Same place, same meaning. Only the language and the voice change. The pairing is clearest in the chorus. In the English version the chorus opens on the Korean whisper 걱정 마… and goes straight on, in the main language, to "I was scared too." In the Korean version the English whisper "Don't worry..." is followed by 나도 무서웠어. The comfort comes first and the confession after it, in the same order in both versions.

The first verse has the same shape. In the English version 괜찮아… sits in front of "Let's take one breath."; in the Korean version "It's okay..." sits in front of 숨 한 번 쉬자. In both, the word that says it's okay comes first.

## A map of all ten songs

![A table of where the whispers sit. Rows are S1A1.01 to 10; columns are Verse 1, Verse 2, Chorus, Bridge, Final Chorus and Outro. Every song has a whisper in Verse 1 and in the Chorus; six songs have one in the Final Chorus, only song 01 has one in Verse 2, and songs 01 and 05 have one in the Bridge. Every Outro holds the same fixed English line, ipe. be still](/assets/journal/whispers-in-the-other-language/whisper-map-ten-letters.jpg)

*Where the 29 whispers sit across the ten songs. The upper line is the Korean in the English-version draft; the lower line is the English in the Korean-version draft.*

Now all ten songs. The English version has 29 whispers and the Korean version has 29. In every one of the ten songs the whispers of the two versions sit in the same section, without a single mismatch.

The positions follow a pattern:

- Verse 1 and Chorus: all ten songs.
- Final Chorus: six songs, 03, 04, 07, 08, 09 and 10. Songs 05 and 06 have a Final Chorus section with no whisper in it, and songs 01 and 02 have no Final Chorus section at all.
- Verse 2: song 01 only.
- Bridge: songs 01 and 05.
- Pre-Chorus: none of the ten.

By song, that is four whispers in track 01, two each in tracks 02 and 06, and three each in the other seven: 4 + 2 + 2 + 7×3 = 29. The baseline is one in the first verse and one in the chorus, with up to two more depending on the song. Why track 01 alone has whispers in both Verse 2 and the Bridge is not on record.

## The most whispered words

![A bar chart of the 29 whispers by phrase. 괜찮아 appears 7 times, 천천히 5 times and 같이 있어 4 times; the other thirteen phrases appear once each. To the right of each bar is the English counterpart used in the Korean-main version](/assets/journal/whispers-in-the-other-language/whisper-vocabulary.jpg)

*The Korean whispers of the English version, counted by phrase. On the right, the English that sits in the same place in the Korean version.*

The 29 whispers are made of 16 different phrases. The most frequent is 괜찮아… ("it's okay") with seven, then 천천히… ("slowly") with five and 같이 있어… ("stay with me") with four. Those three phrases account for 16 of the 29; the other thirteen appear once each.

All seven of the 괜찮아… whispers are in Verse 1: tracks 01, 02, 03, 04, 06, 08 and 09 start their whispering with that word. The other three songs begin differently. Track 05 begins with 천천히…, track 07 with 고마워… ("thank you") and track 10 with 좋은 저녁이야… ("good evening"). The album plan lists a shared vocabulary meant to fix the comforting tone across the record: 괜찮아 / 천천히 / 충분해 / 고마워 / 푹 쉬어. Three of the five, 괜찮아, 천천히 and 고마워, turn up verbatim as whispers. The same register as 충분해 ("enough") appears as 넌 충분히 했어… in track 10. 푹 쉬어 ("rest well") is not a whisper at all; it is a line of main text, 푹 쉬어., in the Outro of the Korean version of track 01.

The English counterparts are not word for word. Of the five 천천히…, four are paired with "Take your time..." and only the one in Verse 1 of track 05 is paired with "Slow down...". Inside track 05, 천천히… appears twice, in Verse 1 and in the Chorus, and the English side uses two different phrases. 숨 쉬어… is paired with "Breathe..." and 그냥 숨 쉬어… with "Just breathe...". So the Korean side has 16 phrases and the English side has 17. The English follows the meaning of the spot more than it follows a dictionary.

## What a whisper points at

Two relationships between a whisper and the line before it can be read. This is our reading of the draft. In the first, the whisper repeats the sense of the line just before it.

In the chorus of track 01 the English version runs:

> Just / breathe, slowly, with me. / 숨 쉬어…

and the Korean version:

> 그냥 / 천천히 숨 쉬자. / Breathe...

The chorus of track 02 does the same. In the English version "sit right here" and "and stay beside me." are followed by 같이 있어…; in the Korean version 여기 앉아서 and 같이 있어도 괜찮아. are followed by "Stay with me...". The other language picks up what the line before has already said.

The chorus of track 07 makes the repetition plain. The English version begins "I'm so glad you're here." and moves through "Today, too," and "I found myself thinking that." before 네가 있어 다행이야… arrives. The Korean version begins 네가 있어서 다행이야. and after 오늘도 and 그 생각을 했어. comes "I'm glad you're here...". The main language has pointed at a thought, and the whisper in the other language states the thought once more.

In the second relationship the whisper offers something the line before did not say. Track 03, whose chorus opens on a whisper, is like that: "Don't worry..." and its Korean twin come first, and the confession follows. The chorus of track 10 sits between the two. In the English version it reads:

> These words — / you'd wished / someone would finally say them. / 넌 충분히 했어…

and in the Korean version 이 말, 누군가는 and 꼭 해줬으면 했잖아. are followed by "You did enough...". Right after the lines point at words someone had hoped to hear, a phrase of that kind is handed over in the whisper of the other language. The draft does not say this; it is the reader's interpretation.

## Reading only the whispers

Read in order, the whispers of a single song make a very short letter. Track 09 gives:

> 괜찮아… / 조금만 더 웃어… / 내일은 올 거야…

In the Korean version the same three places hold "It's okay...", "One more smile..." and "Tomorrow will come...". It starts from it's okay, passes through one more smile and arrives at tomorrow is coming, in seven Korean words. Track 10 goes:

> 좋은 저녁이야… / 넌 충분히 했어… / 잘 자…

with "Good evening...", "You did enough..." and "Sleep well..." in the Korean version. It opens on an evening greeting, passes through the sentence that you did enough, and closes on good night. On the last song of the album the whispers read as a short routine for putting the day away.

No whisper here is long enough to be advice, and most of them simply help you catch your breath. The concept paragraph of the album plan says the album does not push advice, solutions or resolutions on anyone ("조언도, 해결책도, 다짐도 강요하지 않는다"). The same register can be read at the smallest scale here.

## The line that never flips

The rule has one exception. The last line of every song, "ipe. be still", is fixed in English in both versions. In the English version that is the main language, and in the Korean version it is the opposite one. This single line sits in the same spot with the same letters whichever version you play, which is why the Outro column in the map above looks identical on all ten rows.

One more thing to note. In the Outro of the Korean version of track 10, the line right before "ipe. be still" is the English "Good night.", written without a whisper mark; in the English version it is the main language. It falls outside the rule that the opposite language goes in as a whisper, so it is not counted among the 29 above.

The site's own description says that, since the dear(you); album, every track ends on this whispered line. The two songs released outside the album are not part of that count. In the lyric drafts of "still compiling" (N-001) the Outro carries a whisper direction: the section header reads "Outro — 속삭이듯", and the entered lyrics mark the Outro line (whispered) and (속삭이듯). Whether "good morning, world" (U-001) has such marks is something this post did not check. The whisper in the opposite language is a design we can confirm for the ten songs of this album.

## What is not on record

- Why the opposite language was chosen rather than the same one.
- By what criteria and in what order the whisper phrases were picked.
- How large and how long the whispers are on the recordings, and how the opposite-gender harmony sounds.

This post does not write any of these three as if it were on record. What was confirmed is the 29 whispers in the drafts, the places they sit, and the fact that the two versions put the same meaning in the same place.
