In *dear(you); vol.1*, the worry of a developer in the AI era shows up from the very first verse in two songs: song 03, "I Was Scared, Too.", and song 04, "You're Still Shining." (the draft's Verse 1 mentions AI in those two songs only). The album plan names song 07 as part of that emotional center as well, but its Verse 1 has no AI. Song 03 has its own post, [Admitting Fear Instead of Fixing It](/journal/en/i-was-scared-too/), and the one sentence that songs 04 and 07 share in their Bridges is read in [The Same Sentence in Two Songs](/journal/en/same-sentence-in-two-songs/). This post does the job those two leave undone. It puts songs 03 and 04 side by side as a pair and checks, in the lyric drafts, what words and what tense the same worry gets in each.

First, the scope. We read the song design documents (the lyric drafts and the notes) and the album plan. Anything said about sound is a specification from those documents, not an impression from listening. What is actually sung on a recording can be shorter than the draft. And as always: the music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person. A song about the fear of the AI era was made with the help of AI, and we do not hide that.

## The pair, as the documents write it

That the two songs are a pair is written in the documents themselves. The theme field of song 03 begins with the fear that anyone living as a developer in the AI era has felt at least once (our translation). The theme field of song 04 sets its direction from the other side: this song is not a story about "beating AI" (our translation). The design note for song 04 calls it the "answer song" to the previous song, and the brand note says it forms a pair with it, "anxiety ↔ reassurance" (our translation).

So the question for this post is plain. To the fear that song 03 voices, what exactly does song 04 answer inside its lyric? The album plan lists the Key Emotion of song 03 as "anxiety → empathy" and of song 04 as "comfort → reassurance" (our translation). The tempos in the design documents are 72 and 74 BPM.

## The word "developer" never appears

The theme field of song 03 has the word developer. In the lyric drafts of all ten songs, in Korean and in English, the words developer and 개발자 appear zero times. The job shows through the words of the work instead.

![A table of ten songs by six words. The word for developer is 0 in all ten. AI appears twice in song 3, three times in song 4, twice in song 7 and once in song 9. Code appears once each in songs 3, 4, 5 and 7, function once in song 3, review once each in songs 3, 4 and 6, and deploy once in song 4. The rows for songs 3 and 4 are outlined](/assets/journal/fear-and-the-reply/work-words.jpg)

*Counts of word fragments in the Korean-main lyric drafts. "Developer" is 0 in all ten songs.*

Song 03 has function, code and review. Song 04 has code, review and deploy. Song 03's Verse 2 begins like this.

> There was a time I'd stay up all night just to write a single function.

Song 04 has deploy, too, as one line in a list in Verse 2.

> The one who said, after the deploy was done, "You worked hard again today."

Here a profession is drawn as scenes from a day instead of being named. Whether that was intended, or whether a listener who knows those scenes recognizes their own day in them, is not recorded. What the drafts show is that the words are placed this way.

## Fear has a name, and fear has a size

Song 03 calls the fear by name. The Korean draft uses the stem for "scared" twice and the stem for "anxious" twice; the English draft has "it scared me a little", "I was scared too", "Being anxious" and "that anxious heart". In the drafts of song 04, neither word appears even once. What song 04 does instead is measure the fear.

> And for no reason, a corner of my heart grew a little smaller.

The sharpest question in each song has a similar shape. Both begin with "What if". Song 03 asks "What if I'm slowly fading away?" and song 04 asks "What if I'm becoming less and less needed?" Song 03's question reads as being about the self growing faint in time, and song 04's as being about usefulness shrinking. That distinction is a reading of the drafts set next to each other.

Verse 1 of song 04 closes, a thought and a whisper after that question, with this line.

> I felt that way too.

It reads like a reply to the chorus of song 03, "I was scared too." In track order, song 04 starts where the previous song's confession ended, and this time it sets the same worry out as its own.

![Three boxes joined downward. From the top: song 3 Verse 2, "What if I'm slowly fading away?" (Korean: 혹시 내가 천천히 사라지는 건 아닐까.); song 4 Verse 1, "What if I'm becoming less and less needed?" (혹시 나는 점점 필요 없어지는 걸까.); song 4 Outro, "That warmth will never fade away." (그 따뜻함은 절대 사라지지 않을 거야.). At the bottom: song 3 uses the words for scared and anxious four times, and song 4 never does](/assets/journal/fear-and-the-reply/same-verb.jpg)

*Two "What if" questions, and the place in the Outro of song 04 where the same verb turns into a promise.*

The verb is what stands out more. Song 03's fear was spoken with the verb "fade". In song 04 that verb comes back once, with a negation in front of it, and becomes a promise.

> That warmth will never fade away.

The Korean draft takes the same path with its own verb for disappearing: "혹시 내가 천천히 사라지는 건 아닐까" in song 03, and in song 04's Outro "그 따뜻함은 절대 사라지지 않을 거야". The verb of the one who fears vanishing comes back as the verb of something that does not vanish. Because it spans two songs rather than one, it is a link you cannot see by listening to a single song. This too is a reading. No record says the verb was matched on purpose.

## The same two scenes, in a different tense

The two songs share two scenes: smiling at a colleague who failed and telling them it's okay, and leaving one more review late at night. In song 03 they come in the later part of the Bridge. In song 04 they come in Verse 2. The two examples in song 03's Bridge and the closing sentence of song 04 are also quoted in [The Same Sentence in Two Songs](/journal/en/same-sentence-in-two-songs/); what this post adds is the contrast in tense.

![Two panels. On the left, song 3 Bridge: "Smiling at a colleague who failed, telling them it's okay. Leaving one more review late at night. That's still something only a person can do." On the right, song 4 Verse 2: "The one who left one more review late into the night. The one who smiled first at a colleague who slipped, saying it's okay. The one who said, after the deploy was done, 'You worked hard again today.' That wasn't the AI. It was always a person." Captions give the Korean drafts](/assets/journal/fear-and-the-reply/two-scenes.jpg)

*In song 03 the two scenes are examples of what a person can do. In song 04 they are the people who did it today. The picture shows the Korean drafts.*

Song 03 phrases the scenes as things a person can do: "Smiling at a colleague who failed… Leaving one more review late at night." It speaks in general, not about one particular day, and it closes like this.

> That's still something only a person can do.

Song 04 phrases them as people who did it: "The one who left one more review… The one who smiled first…". It takes the same acts out of a day that has already passed, and it adds one more to the list, the one who said well done after the deploy. The closing sentence opens with the same word, "that", but says something else.

> That wasn't the AI. It was always a person.

Song 03 sets up the human share with "still … can", a word of possibility. Song 04 sets it up with "always", a word that looks back over what happened. One claim sits first as a possibility and then as a confirmation. The Korean draft marks the difference in how the lines end, a general "…는 것도" in song 03 against a past "…ㄴ 사람" in song 04.

## What comes after "But"

The Final Choruses of both songs carry the same premise: tomorrow the world gets faster. Song 03 says it and goes straight to permission.

> Tomorrow, the world will grow faster still. But that's okay.

Song 04 adds a line to the premise and speaks a fact rather than a permission.

> The world will grow faster still tomorrow. New models, too, will keep on coming. But even so, what makes a person smile is, in the end, a person.

After "but" in song 03 come a permission to the listener and an identity: the one who makes someone else's tomorrow a little warmer. After "but" in song 04 comes one sentence about people in general. Still, the two Final Choruses share one line.

> Today, too, you were enough.

In song 03 that line comes before the talk of speed, and in song 04 after it. Song 03 sets the premise next and closes with the identity sentence. Song 04 sets the premise first and closes with this line and "Today, too, you're still shining."

The Pre-Choruses split in a similar way. Song 03 reads the anxiety itself again: being anxious does not mean you have given up, it means you still hold it dear. Song 04 resets what speed is measured against.

> the fast ones don't always last the longest. The ones who leave their heart behind are the ones remembered longer.

Song 03 gives the fear a meaning, and song 04 sets the heart you leave behind against speed. That is an interpretation of the two songs set side by side.

## What the two songs do not say

The story field of song 04 describes a day in which, in a fast-changing world, you keep comparing yourself with AI (our translation). Yet in the Korean drafts, none of the words for beating, competing or comparing appear in either song. The songs give the size of the heart in which a comparison happens ("a little smaller", "less and less needed"). They do set speeds side by side, though: the chorus of song 03 ("the one who walks to the very end lasts longer than the one who runs the fastest") and the Pre-Chorus of song 04 ("the fast ones don't always last the longest").

Nor does the lyric say what to learn. Song 03 has "The things to learn were quietly piling up." and "You, still learning today, are doing more than enough." But learning is a situation, never a method, and not one line says how. In the draft of song 04 the word for learning does not appear at all. The story field of song 03 asks whether I will be okay from here on, says the song does not answer that question, and says that instead it tells the person who feels this that they are not alone (our translation). Song 04, rather than offering an answer, counts inside the lyric what the person who asked the question did today. That sentence is a reading. The design documents do not say it.

After these two, the word AI carries on. It sits in a list in song 09 ("New technology, new AI, and yet another change — all of it waiting for us") and in the matching list line of song 07 ("New technology, new AI, grows more amazing every day"), and song 07 also has it in the Bridge sentence it shares with song 04. It can be read as AI sitting in more places as one of the things the world brings; no record says so.

## The sound is close; the instrument fields differ a little

The specifications of the two songs are close. Song 03 is Ambient Lo-fi and Emotional Chill at 72 BPM, and song 04 is Ambient Lo-fi and Warm Chill at 74 BPM. In the instrument fields, song 03 lists Soft Piano, Warm Electric Piano, Gentle Guitar, Vinyl Noise, Soft Rain Texture and Ambient Pads, and song 04 lists Soft Piano, Warm Guitar, Gentle Strings, Soft Pads, Vinyl Noise and Rain Ambience. The album plan groups songs 03 and 04 as Emotional Chill with gentle strings coming in, but only song 04's instrument field lists strings ("Gentle Strings"); song 03's lists guitar and piano but not strings. How that difference sounds on the recordings, we have not checked.

## What is not recorded

- Why the two songs were set as a pair. The notes say only "answer song" and "pair", not why.
- Whether the matching "What if" questions or the returning verb "fade" were intended. What this post read is the shape on the page.
- Which of songs 03 and 04 was written first. The documents have number order only, and nothing we read proves the order of writing.
- Any real reaction from developers, or survey figures. This post quotes none.
- How this contrast sounds on the recordings.
