"It's okay" is the album's most familiar phrase. It is in the title of the second song, "It's Okay, Take Your Time.", and in the title of the fifth, "It's Okay to Rest a While." It is also the first thing a reader might expect to find everywhere. This post counts it in the lyric sheets of all ten songs and checks where it actually sits.

The scope and method first. We read the lyric sheets in the ten song design documents (the English version and the Korean version of each). The documents were written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ, and we did not check the counts against the audio. Whispered lines, which are in the other language, were skipped, and titles were not counted. In English we counted the word "okay" only (so "all right" is not counted). In Korean we counted every word containing 괜찮 (괜찮아, 괜찮은, 괜찮냐고, 괜찮다고 and so on). The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## The counts

| Song | English "okay" | Korean 괜찮 |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 3 | 6 |
| 02 | 2 | 6 |
| 03 | 6 | 6 |
| 04 | 4 | 4 |
| 05 | 5 | 5 |
| 06 | 5 | 6 |
| 07 | 3 | 4 |
| 08 | 4 | 5 |
| 09 | 2 | 4 |
| 10 | 4 | 3 |
| Total | 38 | 49 |

Song 03, "I Was Scared, Too.", has the most in English, six. In Korean, songs 01, 02, 03 and 06 tie at six. The lowest English counts are songs 02 and 09 with two each. The lowest Korean count is song 10, the last song, with three. It is the only song where the Korean number is lower than the English one.

## It lives in the chorus and the bridge

Summed over the ten songs, the English counts by section are: Chorus 12, Bridge 8, Verse 2 6, Final Chorus 5, Verse 1 4, Pre-Chorus 3, Outro 0. The Korean counts are Chorus 15, Bridge 11, Verse 1 8, Verse 2 8, Final Chorus 4, Pre-Chorus 2, Outro 1. Sections differ in length, so these are raw counts. Even so, the pattern is the opposite of the one in [Counting "Today" in Ten Songs](/journal/en/the-word-today-in-ten-songs/), where every Outro contains the word. No English Outro says "okay" at all. The only Korean Outro that does is song 01's, with 「천천히 와도 괜찮아.」

## Permission, not report

In English, 17 of the 38 uses take the shape "okay to ..." (It's okay to rest, to be late, to be a little slow). They appear in every song except 09. The counts per song, from 01 to 10, are 1, 1, 3, 1, 3, 3, 1, 2, 0, 2. Song 09 uses "okay" as a condition instead: "if tomorrow is hard too, that's okay", and "doesn't change, it's okay."

There is a second use that is a question. Songs 04 and 07 both contain "are you okay?" in English. In Korean the matching forms are 괜찮냐고 (asking whether someone is okay), which appears once each in songs 04 and 07. Here "okay" is not an allowance but the thing a person asks first.

## The Korean "pretending to be okay"

Three Korean sheets, songs 01, 02 and 06, have a Verse 1 line with 괜찮은 척 ("pretending to be okay"): 「괜찮은 척 웃었지만」 in 01 and 02 and 「괜찮은 척 웃었지.」 in 06. In song 06 the same verse then quotes the stock answer, 「"괜찮습니다."」, and goes on to 「솔직해져도 괜찮아.」, so the mask and the permission sit a few lines apart. In song 02 a plain 「괜찮아.」 comes just before the mask line. In song 01 the later uses include permissions (「오늘은 조금 늦어도 괜찮아.」, 「아무 말 하지 않아도 괜찮아.」). We note only the placement in the sheets. We did not check whether the English sheets mirror it, and the design documents do not say it was designed this way.

## The shape of the Korean word

Of the 49 Korean uses, 32 are the plain form 「괜찮아.」 The others are 괜찮은 (4), 괜찮다고 (4), 괜찮지 (2), 괜찮냐고 (2) and five single forms. The word is mostly said, not described.

## What we are not claiming

- Whether the word was spread this way on purpose is not in the design documents.
- The counts come from the 2026-07-04 design documents, not the audio, and the sung lyrics may differ.
- Counting the word says nothing about whether a listener is, or feels, okay. We make no claim about health or effect.
- We counted only the word "okay", not every phrase of reassurance.
