*The ten songs by emotion name and design-document tempo. Four songs come before track 05 and five come after.*

*dear(you); vol.1* is a ten-song album, and in its track list the album plan puts the word "center" next to track 05, "It's Okay to Rest a While." (잠깐 쉬어가도 돼.). This post follows that word into the lyric drafts: what can be confirmed there and what cannot. We look at the sentences it shares with its neighbours, a chorus that is noticeably short, and the first "we" in the Korean drafts. The pairing of track 01 and track 10 is covered in [an earlier post](/journal/en/open-with-a-question-close-with-comfort/); here the focus is on how the song that sits between them connects to both.

Some limits first. The revision notes for track 05 are empty, and the memo in its song design document records only the composition request and the song's place in the album, so this post reads only the structure written in the lyric drafts, the song design document and the album plan, and does not claim to know why anything was done. What we say about sound is taken from the specifications in the design documents; it is not an impression from listening. What is actually sung can also be shorter than the draft. And, as always: the music is produced with AI (Suno). Lyrics are AI-drafted and reviewed by a person.

## Where is the middle?

Ten songs have no single middle song. Four come before track 05 and five come after. Even so, the album plan lists its character as "setting the breath down (center)" (our translation), and the memo in track 05's design document calls it the center of the emotional arc. Neither document spells out whether "center" means position in the running order or the middle of the emotional movement. This post reads it as the second, but that is a choice on the reader's side.

The album plan's arc runs: empathy, understanding, anxiety, reassurance, rest, connection, gratitude, acknowledgement, hope, peace. "Rest" is the emotion name of track 05 alone, and the last song is named for peace, not rest. By the design-document tempos, track 05 is 66 to 70 BPM. Among the values listed for tracks 01 to 09, 66 is the lowest, and only track 10 goes lower, at 64. The theme field of the design document puts the song in one line (our translation):

> A story that resting is courage too.

The word "courage" does not appear in any of the ten lyric drafts, Korean or English. The courage the theme field names is not repeated as a word in the lyric; as we will see below, it only shows up as lines being set down one by one.

## From the last line of 04 to the title of 05

Track 04's Outro ends like this.

> For today, you can rest now.

Track 05's Chorus opens with "It's okay to rest a while," and the Korean title, 잠깐 쉬어가도 돼, is also the first line of its Chorus. The permission that closed track 04 comes back, in different words, as the title and the opening line of the next song's Chorus. The Korean is close but not identical: 오늘은 이제 쉬어도 돼 at the end of 04, 잠깐 쉬어가도 돼 for 05. Track 04 is the "reassurance" song in the album plan, so the order is reassurance first, and rest at the end of it. The Bridge sentence that tracks 04 and 07 share word for word is read in [a separate post](/journal/en/same-sentence-in-two-songs/).

The Outro greeting is the interesting part. In the Korean drafts, the Outros of tracks 01 to 04 all say 고마워, "thank you." Track 04's reads:

> Thank you for making someone's day a little warmer today too.

Track 05's Outro has no word of thanks, and its English draft has no "Thank you" either. Counting every section, only tracks 05 and 09 have no word of thanks anywhere in the song, and "thank you" returns in track 06's Outro. What track 05's Outro offers instead is "Rest well." You could read it as a song about resting that does not collect a greeting in return, but why thanks was left out is not on record, so that is one possible reading. What the drafts confirm is that this song skips a greeting the four before it gave every time.

## The evening of 01, turned over

![Two pairs of cards setting the lyrics of tracks 01 and 05 side by side. Above, track 01 Verse 2, the coffee had gone cold, the outside went dark unnoticed, and the laptop was opened again, leads to track 05 Verse 1, rain falling slowly outside the window and steam rising from the warm coffee, and the Bridge, slowly close the laptop. Below, the unread alerts of track 01 Verse 1 that quietly pushed the day forward lead to the unread mail and unfinished code of track 05 Verse 2, the kind that know how to wait a little](/assets/journal/rest-at-the-center/mirrored-evening.jpg)

*The evening scene of track 01, turned over one object at a time in track 05. The drafts are read side by side; the picture says nothing about order of writing or intent.*

The most visible thing in track 05 is that the props of track 01 come back almost unchanged. Verse 2 of track 01 lays out cold coffee, a window that went dark unnoticed and a laptop opened again.

> Your coffee had gone cold somewhere along the way, and you didn't even notice when the sky outside had turned dark.

Verse 1 of track 05 has the same two objects in a different state.

> Outside the window, the rain is falling slowly, and from the warm coffee, steam is quietly rising.

The cold coffee now gives off steam, and the window that went unnoticed is something to look at. In track 01 the wish to rest was pushed down like this.

> You wanted a little rest, but afraid you'd fall behind, you opened up the laptop again.

Track 05's Bridge uses another verb for the same laptop.

> Shall we slowly close the laptop?

"Opened up" looks back at something already done; "shall we close" offers something still to come. In the Korean drafts, the 잠깐 ("for a moment") in track 01's 잠깐 쉬고 싶었는데, "I wanted to rest for a moment," becomes the first word of track 05's title. The alerts of track 01's Verse 1 move too.

> all the alerts you left unread quietly pushed the whole day forward.

In Verse 2 of track 05 they have become mail and code, and the things that pushed the day forward are now the ones that wait.

> The unread mail, the unfinished code — they're the kind that know how to wait a little.

The "this far" lines point the same way. Track 01 closes its Chorus with "For today, this far is enough." Track 05 says "For today, this far is more than enough" in the second and third lines of its Chorus, and its Bridge says it once more, "This day of ours — this far is enough." In the Korean, 여기까지면 충분해 in track 01 becomes 여기까지만 해도 괜찮아 in track 05.

What is confirmed here is that the props overlap. Nothing on record says track 05 was meant to flip track 01. It only reads that way when the two are put side by side. If track 01 looks back on a day with no rest, track 05 reads as saying you may rest in the same evening. The fears are not the same. In track 01 it was falling behind. In track 05 the Pre-Chorus says "Pausing for a moment doesn't mean you've lost your way." and the fear being soothed is getting lost.

## The window shows up three times

The window appears in track 01 as well, but in track 05 it appears in three different slots: at the start, in the middle and at the end. Verse 1 opens with the window, the Chorus passes through it ("It's okay to gaze out the window and smile at nothing at all"), and the Outro closes on it.

> As the lights outside the window grow a little dim, I hope today's worries, too, drift slowly away.

The story field of the design document sums the song up as a time for looking out the window, drinking a cup of coffee and listening to music without thinking about anything. The lyric has the window and the coffee as they are. In the Korean drafts no other song names the window three times. The lights of the Outro lead into track 10's Verse 1, where the English draft reads "The lights outside the window, one by one, are slowly falling asleep." The darkness no one noticed in track 01, the lights that dim in track 05 and the lights that go to sleep in track 10 are what [the earlier post](/journal/en/open-with-a-question-close-with-comfort/) tracked between 01 and 10, with track 05 sitting in the middle of that line. This order is a reading of the drafts placed side by side as well.

## Four lines of waiting, and the first "we"

The Korean lyric of track 05 has four lines containing 기다, "wait," more than any other of the ten songs, where no other song has more than two. Laid out in order, the one who waits keeps changing.

> The things you have to do will still be waiting tomorrow.

> they're the kind that know how to wait a little.

> you too — wait for yourself.

> The world will wait for you longer than you think.

Tasks wait, then the mail and the code, then comes a request that you wait for yourself, and in the end the world waits. In the English draft, five lines carry wait or waiting (counting "Wait a moment…" in Verse 1), and no other song has more than two. After those lines, the last sentence of the Outro arrives.

> Because tomorrow, we can meet again, slowly.

In the Korean drafts this is where 우리, "we," first appears. Tracks 01 to 04 contain none of 우리, 우린 or 우릴. The last permission in track 01's Outro was this.

> Tomorrow, you can come slowly too.

A sentence in which the listener is the one who comes has become a sentence where two people meet, and "slowly" is kept in both. The earlier post read the move from "come" to "meet" between tracks 01 and 10, and its first step turns out to have been taken once already, in the middle song. The same frame is in track 08's Final Chorus ("Because tomorrow, we can walk on again") and in track 10's Verse 2 ("Because tomorrow, we can meet again"), and in the Korean it is only track 05 that has 천천히 ("slowly") where tracks 08 and 10 have 다시 ("again"). The English drafts are freer translations and differ here: the Bridge of the English draft of track 02 already says "we." So this talk of a first appearance has to be limited to the Korean drafts.

## A short Chorus, a long Bridge

![A horizontal bar chart comparing the number of Chorus lines in the Korean drafts of the ten songs. 01 has 12, 02 has 11, 03 has 14, 04 has 13, 05 has 9, 06 has 11, and 07 to 10 have 10 each. Whole-song line counts are 01 59, 02 57, 03 82, 04 82, 05 68, 06 78, 07 69, 08 80, 09 70 and 10 74. Only 05 is highlighted in yellow](/assets/journal/rest-at-the-center/short-chorus.jpg)

*Chorus lines in the Korean drafts. Track 05's nine lines are the fewest of the ten. Counts leave out the title and blank lines and include whispers and the closing signature.*

The album plan lists "the chorus hook is the title" (our translation) among its repeating devices, and track 05's Chorus does open on its title sentence. Set against the other nine, it is the shortest. Its Chorus is nine lines in both the Korean and the English draft. The ten Korean Choruses run 12, 11, 14, 13, 9, 11, 10, 10, 10 and 10 lines.

The whole song is short too. Seven-slot songs run from track 03 to track 10, and their Korean line counts are 82, 82, 68, 78, 69, 80, 70 and 74, so track 05's 68 is the fewest. In English the counts are 82, 80, 67, 79, 68, 78, 70 and 73, and track 05's 67 is again the fewest. Track 03 is where the [Final Chorus first appears](/journal/en/i-was-scared-too/), and track 05's Final Chorus has the same nine lines as its Chorus.

The longest slot in the song is the Bridge, at 13 lines in Korean, and that is where the laptop and the phone are.

> And turn the phone face down for a little while?

Together with "Shall we slowly close the laptop?" it is an offer, not an order. The Korean ends both lines with a full stop, not a question mark. In the Korean draft the only line with a question mark in track 05 is in Verse 1.

> would you sit here with me?

Track 01 asked "wasn't it?" twice. Track 05 asks once, and it is an invitation, not a request for confirmation. In English, three lines end with a question mark: the invitation to sit, the laptop and the phone. Why the Chorus is short and the Bridge long is not on record; we counted lines and that is all we say. The whispers of this song sit in three places, Verse 1, Chorus and Bridge, and not in the Final Chorus, which [the post on whispers](/journal/en/whispers-in-the-other-language/) lays out.

## The same request, twice in the middle

Requests built around 너 자신, "yourself," appear in the Korean drafts of tracks 02, 04, 05 and 08.

- Track 02, Pre-Chorus: 너 자신에게도 조금 다정해져 줘 — "be a little gentle with yourself too"
- Track 04, Chorus: 너 자신을 조금 더 믿어줘 — "believe in yourself just a little more"
- Track 05, Verse 2: 너도 너 자신을 기다려줘 — "you too — wait for yourself"
- Track 05, Bridge: 이제는 너 자신을 조금 더 아껴줘 — "be a little more tender with yourself"
- Track 08, Final Chorus: 너 자신을 조금만 안아줘 — "Just hold, a little, the one who lived through today"
- Track 08, Outro: 너 자신에게도 고생했다고 말해줘 — "tell yourself, too"

That is six in all, and two of them are in track 05. Counted in order, they are the third and fourth of six, the two in the middle. The verbs move on: be gentle, trust, wait, cherish, hold, tell. Track 05's two requests are about waiting and cherishing, and neither asks you to achieve anything. The number is only a count; there is no record that track 05 was placed at the middle of the six on purpose.

## One rest, two "beautiful"s

The Pre-Chorus of track 05 holds an image borrowed from music.

> Just as a song is beautiful only because of its rests, the time you take to rest is part of your day too.

The Korean puts it as 쉼표가 있어야 노래가 아름답듯, and in English "rests" (the musical kind) and "rest" (the verb) sit side by side in one sentence. The word "beautiful" appears in the lyric of track 05 and no other song, in the Korean (아름) and the English drafts alike, and it appears twice. One is the line above, and the other is the last line of the Final Chorus.

> Today, just by holding on so well, it was a beautiful day, more than enough.

If it is the rests that make a song beautiful, then in this song what makes a day beautiful is having held on. The two lines pair up inside one song. Nothing on record says this was intended. What is confirmed is that, on an album that puts rest at its center, one line inside the center song compares rest to a musical rest.

## Sound, as specified

The specifications run as follows. In the song design document, track 05 is Ambient Lo-fi and Sleep Lo-fi at 66 to 70 BPM, with a mood of Peaceful, Warm, Calm and Cozy. The instruments and textures listed are Soft Felt Piano, Warm Nylon Guitar, Gentle Ambient Pads, Vinyl Crackle, Soft Rain and Low Fireplace Texture. The mix field names Warm Analog Tone, Wide Stereo, Long Soft Reverb and Very Gentle Compression. The sound arc in the album plan describes track 05 as "Sleep Lo-fi — the quietest, fireplace and rain, setting the breath down completely." (our translation)

Set the ten design documents side by side and a few things stand out. The genre name Sleep Lo-fi is in the genre field of tracks 05 and 10 only, and the fireplace texture is in the instrument field of track 05 alone. The "the rain is falling slowly" of the lyric and the Soft Rain of the instrument field also line up. But these are values written in the plan and the design document. How the difference sounds on the recordings is not something this post asserts, and we have not checked whether track 05 actually sounds the quietest of the ten.

## A song you can hear alone

The songs came out as singles, one at a time, so some listeners may hear only track 05. Its theme field says only that it is a song that tells you, however busy the world keeps moving, that for today it is fine to stop for a while, and attaches no condition about the earlier songs. So without the joins pointed out in this post, "It's okay to rest a while" should read where it stands. For someone who has heard the songs before it, the same words read one layer deeper, as the permission at the end of track 04 carried on, and that layer is something the reader adds. The whole of track 01 is in [the post on the first song](/journal/en/check-in-before-you-fix/).

## What is not on record

A list of what this post deliberately does not say.

- Why track 05 sits at the center of the album. What is confirmed is only that the album plan writes "center."
- Why the Chorus is the shortest of the ten, and why thanks was left out of the Outro.
- The order in which track 05's lyric was written and how it was revised. The revision notes are empty.
- How the recording sounds. The specifications above are design-document values.
