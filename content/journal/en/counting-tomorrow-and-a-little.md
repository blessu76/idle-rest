The ninth song of *dear(you); vol.1* is called "Let's Smile a Little Tomorrow." (Korean 「내일은 조금 웃자.」). Its title holds two small words, "tomorrow" and "a little", and both can be counted across the whole album. This post counts them in all ten lyric sheets and looks at where they cluster.

The scope and method come first. We read the lyric sheets in the ten song design documents (an English version and a Korean version for each). The documents were written on 2026-07-04 when the songs were commissioned, so what is sung may differ, and we did not check the counts against the audio. Whispered lines, which are in the other language, were skipped. In English we counted the whole word "tomorrow" and the phrase "a little". In Korean we counted every word containing 내일 (내일은, 내일도, 내일의) and every word containing 조금 (조금 더, 조금만). The music is made with AI (Suno); the lyrics are drafted by AI and reviewed by a person.

## Tomorrow belongs to the last two songs

Songs 09 and 10 together hold 11 of the 21 English "tomorrow" and 11 of the 23 Korean 내일. Song 07 has none in either version. Songs 01, 02, 04 and 06 have exactly one each in both versions. The word is present in nine of the ten songs, and in the Outro of seven of them (01, 02, 03, 05, 06, 09 and 10, in both languages). Song 04 and song 08 use it only in the Final Chorus.

It is not always hopeful. In song 04 the word describes the world: "The world / will grow faster still tomorrow." In song 05 it describes the to-do list: "The things you have to do / will still be waiting tomorrow." In the Outros it often turns into a wish: song 02's Outro has the line "I hope tomorrow". Song 09 is the one that builds its chorus on it ("Tomorrow, / let's smile a little."), and its Outro ends with "See you tomorrow." (Korean 「내일 만나.」).

## "A little" belongs to song 09

Song 09 says "a little" 12 times in English and 조금 14 times in Korean. The next highest song, 05, has 7 in both languages. In English the 12 split like this: Verse 1 two, Verse 2 two, Chorus four, Bridge one, Final Chorus two, Outro one.

The interesting part is that the same phrase measures two different things. In Verse 1 it measures how tired the day was: "A little worn, / a little shaken," (Korean 「조금 지쳤고, / 조금 흔들렸고,」). In Verse 2 it asks permission to be late: "would it be so bad / to walk a little late?" The chorus then turns it into the thing to do: "let's smile a little", "Let's breathe a little deeper", "a little warmer". The bridge says "smile a little more / the day after," and the Outro wishes "a day a little gentler." The same small unit is used for the tiredness and for the hoped-for smile, and the chorus asks only for "Just a little / more than today."

## What song 10 does with it

Song 10 is high on "tomorrow" (five in English, six in Korean) but low on "a little" (four in each). It is also the slowest song of the album at 64 BPM, as [Closing the Album with Its Slowest Song](/journal/en/closing-with-the-slowest-song/) shows. We only note the counts side by side. The design documents do not say whether the pairing was planned.

## What we are not claiming

- Whether the clustering of these words in songs 09 and 10 was planned is not in the design documents.
- The counts come from the 2026-07-04 design documents, not the audio, and the sung lyrics may differ.
- "A little" and 조금 are counted as phrases, not as meanings. We did not try to sort every use into a theme.
- Nothing here says what a listener will feel.
